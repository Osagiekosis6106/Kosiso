# America Memory — "When America Made Everything" Style

Trigger phrase: **"america memory"**. Also load this file whenever the user says they want to model **When America Made Everything** (or a lost-American-brands / factory-towns / nostalgia channel). Follow every rule below when writing scripts, beat lists, image prompts or video prompts.

Reference channel: When America Made Everything (@WhenAmericaMadeEverything, channel ID `UCgYTGQaaVl--ma4BOX41-MA`). Launched Sep 2, 2026; 727K views and ~4.8K subs in 3 weeks; est. RPM $12–17; ~64-min list videos; audience = US viewers aged 55–75 sharing memories in the comments.

The user is a busy mom. Keep every workflow short, batchable and copy-paste ready.

---

## 1. Video format

- **Length:** start at 30–40 min (the reference does 56–71 min). One upload per week.
- **Titles:** `[25/30] American [Companies/Factories/Brands/Towns] That [emotional past] — [Then Vanished / Now Gone Forever / Until the Jobs Disappeared]`
  - e.g. "30 American Companies That Made Products That Lasted 50 Years — Then Vanished"
- **Hook (first ~45 s):**
  1. Specific tragic anecdote (e.g. the day a named factory closed) (0–12 s)
  2. Counter-intuitive claim: "Most people think these companies died because their products got worse. The truth is stranger." (12–20 s)
  3. Tease two entries without naming them (20–32 s)
  4. Subscribe ask + promise: "30 American companies whose products lasted 50 years… then vanished." (32–45 s)
- **Each list entry (60–120 s):** number + name → the golden age (why people loved it) → the turning point / bad decision → the end (date closed or sold) → one-line moral ("The product never failed.")
- **Voice:** calm, deep, slow, respectful; middle-aged male in the reference. Pauses on dates and names.
- **Finish on every visual:** film grain + dust/scratches, light vignette, slow Ken Burns zoom/pan on stills, occasional orange light leak, new visual every 3–5 s, low cinematic strings/synth music, big semi-transparent entry number (#30) on screen.

## 2. Visual mix (measured from the reference channel)

The reference's best video was ~80% real archival, 10% AI, 5% modern stock, 5% graphics. A weaker one was ~38% archival / 37% AI / 25% graphics.

**Target for the user:**

| Type | Share |
|---|---|
| Real archival photos + film + modern stock | **≥ 55%** (aim for 65%) |
| AI images / AI motion | **≤ 25%** |
| Graphics (numbers, timelines, maps) | the rest (~10–15%) |

Mostly-AI videos risk demonetization under YouTube's mass-produced/inauthentic content rules. Real footage also builds trust with this audience.

## 3. Beat-tagging rules (the core of this memory)

When turning a script into visuals, split it into beats of 3–5 seconds (about 8–14 spoken words each). Give **every beat exactly one tag**, decided in this order:

| # | If the beat… | Tag | Where to get it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | names a **real** company, factory, town, person, product, event or date **and shows action** (workers moving, trains, crowds, machines) | `🎞️ ARCHIVAL FILM` | Prelinger / National Archives |
| 2 | names a **real** company, factory, town, person, product, event or date (still moment) | `📷 ARCHIVAL PHOTO` | Library of Congress / National Archives / NYPL / Wikimedia |
| 3 | shows an **old advertisement, catalog or product** | `📰 ARCHIVAL AD` | Pre-1931 scans (Internet Archive, Smithsonian, NYPL). If none exists, fall back to `🎨 AI IMAGE` with **no logo** and add the brand name as a text overlay |
| 4 | talks about **today / what's left now** (empty lots, abandoned plants, quiet main streets, drone views) | `🎬 MODERN STOCK` | Pexels / Pixabay / Mixkit |
| 5 | contains **numbers, dates, money, a timeline, a map, a comparison or a list number** | `🔢 GRAPHIC` | CapCut text / timeline / map template |
| 6 | is **generic mood or atmosphere** with no specific real subject: "imagine…", unnamed families, a lunch pail, a kitchen at dawn; **or** any beat where rules 1–4 would probably find nothing | `🎨 AI IMAGE` (+ optional `🎥 AI MOTION`) | ChatGPT / Gemini / Midjourney / Ideogram, then Kling / Veo / Runway for motion |

**Hard rules**
- **Never** use AI for a real named person, a real brand logo or sign, or anything presented as a real photo of a specific real event. AI garbles lettering (e.g. "PACKARD" signs); put brand names in as a CapCut text overlay instead.
- No more than 3 AI beats in a row. Break runs up with a real photo or a graphic.
- Every real/stock beat gets a **fallback**: a second search, or an AI prompt, in case the first search finds nothing.
- If AI images look like real historical photos, remind the user to tick YouTube's **"altered or synthetic content" = Yes** when uploading.

## 5. Free source directory (safe, public domain or free licence)

| Source | Best for | Collections / search tips |
|---|---|---|
| **Library of Congress** (loc.gov/free-to-use; loc.gov/pictures) | Photos 1880s–1940s: factories, towns, workers, stores | **FSA/OWI** 1935–44 (175K photos, public domain); **Detroit Publishing Co.** (factories/towns c.1900–1920); **HABS/HAER** (industrial buildings, mills, plants); Bain News; Harris & Ewing. Look for "No known restrictions on publication." |
| **Prelinger Archives** (archive.org/details/prelinger) | **Moving film**: assembly lines, 1950s homes and kitchens, company promo films, old commercials | Search product or industry words (`steel`, `refrigerator`, `assembly line`, `department store`, `kitchen 1950s`). Download the MP4 and trim 3–5 s clips. |
| **National Archives** (catalog.archives.gov) | US-government photos and film (public domain) | WWII war production (Office of War Information); **DOCUMERICA 1971–77** (colour photos of 1970s America: factory towns, pollution, main streets; good for decline eras) |
| **Smithsonian Open Access** (si.edu/openaccess) | Old products, trade catalogs, ads | Filter to **CC0** only |
| **NYPL Digital Collections** (digitalcollections.nypl.org) | Stores, streets, ads, menus | Tick "public domain materials only" |
| **Internet Archive texts** (archive.org) | Old Sears/Montgomery Ward catalogs, trade magazines | Only **pre-1931** pages are certainly public domain |
| **Wikimedia Commons** (commons.wikimedia.org) | Company-specific photos, factory exteriors today | Check **every** file's licence; credit if CC BY |
| **Flickr Commons** (flickr.com/commons) | Museum/library photos | Only "No known copyright restrictions" |
| **Pexels / Pixabay / Mixkit** | Modern drone and abandoned-building footage | Free licences (not public domain), fine for YouTube |

**Never use:** Getty, Alamy, Shutterstock, British Pathé, CriticalPast, Periscope Film (all paid), screenshots of other YouTube videos, or random Google Images.

**Content ID tip:** some companies wrongly claim public-domain footage. Keep the sourcing log; if a video gets claimed, dispute it as "public domain" and paste the source link.

**Public-domain rule of thumb (as of 2026):** anything published in the US in **1930 or earlier** is public domain; anything made by the **US federal government** is public domain.

## 6. AI prompt style

**Black & white era photo (default):**
```
[decade] black and white documentary photograph, [scene], [people/action], [light], heavy film grain, slight softness, 35mm Kodak Tri-X, candid, no text, no logos
```

**Faded colour 1950s–60s:**
```
1950s Kodachrome colour photograph, faded warm colours, [scene], [people/action], natural light, film grain, candid snapshot, no text, no logos
```

**Today / decay (only if no stock clip exists):**
```
present-day documentary photograph, abandoned [factory/main street/plant] in [region], overcast light, weeds through concrete, broken windows, muted colours, no text, no logos
```

**AI motion (image-to-video, 5 s):** always minimal motion:
```
very slow push-in / slow pan, [one small movement: steam rising, smoke drifting, a figure walking slowly], no morphing, keep faces and hands stable, film grain, 24 fps
```

Avoid close-ups of hands and faces (AI's weak spot), readable signs, and brand logos.

## 7. Thumbnail style

Vintage illustrated poster look: warm oranges and browns, a brick factory or storefront with a water tower, 1–2 nostalgic figures (worker, 1950s housewife), a big bold banner title at the top ("BUILT TO LAST, THEN GONE", "LOST FACTORIES", "FACTORY TOWNS THAT DIED"). No real logos.
