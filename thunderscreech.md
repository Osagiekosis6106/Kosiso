## Hook and outline (target ≈1,500 words ≈ 10 min)

**Myth-buster hook (our edge):** the internet says "900 sonic booms per second". The physics says otherwise: 3 blades × ~2,100 rpm ÷ 60 = **~105 blade passes a second** (⚠ VERIFY rpm and blade count). Nobody in the niche has done the arithmetic on screen.

1. **Cold open (0:00–0:40):** Edwards, 1955. Crews tow a fighter miles out onto the dry lake before they dare start it. "Republic XF-84H Thunderscreech" said aloud in the first 30 s.
2. **The problem (0:40–1:45):** jets were slow to spool up and thirsty; the Navy and Air Force wanted jet speed with propeller response for carriers.
3. **The answer (1:45–2:40):** an F-84F rebuilt around a ~5,850 shp Allison turboprop and a 12-ft propeller.
4. **X-RAY: the propeller that never slowed down (2:40–4:00):** constant rpm, thrust set by blade pitch, so the tips were supersonic even at idle. Shock cones forming at the tips.
5. **The arithmetic (4:00–4:45):** 105 vs "900". What a tip at Mach 1.18 actually means.
6. **Built crooked to fly straight (4:45–5:30):** offset wing leading edges and the T-tail against torque and propeller wash.
7. **On the ground (5:30–6:30):** nausea, headaches, the tower and the light gun. Only claims that pass the fact-check.
8. **In the air (6:30–7:45):** Hank Beaird, Lin Hendrix (one flight), 12 flights / 11 emergency landings, vibration, nose-wheel and engine problems.
9. **Cancelled (7:45–8:30):** jets got better; the problem it solved went away.
10. **What survived (8:30–9:15):** the surviving airframe in Dayton; supersonic-tip propellers in later turboprops (Tu-95).
11. **Pogo tease (9:15–9:45):** "The same engine family tried something even stranger: standing on its tail."
